System Architecture & Pipeline Overview

Foundational design philosophy, live data ingestion, and chronological execution sequence.

1. Data Ingestion & Live API Infrastructure

The engine ingests authentic play-by-play and game summary data directly from the College Football Data API (CFBD) via automated endpoints (/games and /teams/fbs).

The engine evaluates college football calendar seasons dynamically: when executing during January or February (e.g., the National Championship Game in January 2027), the pipeline automatically maps post-New Year postseason matchups directly into the preceding fall campaign (2026) without data fragmentation.

2. Elimination of Subjective Bias & Pure Parity

Subjective human voter polls (AP Top 25, Coaches Poll) carry zero mathematical weight in this system. Furthermore, conference baseline strength is completely excluded from active in-season Pure Elo, preventing mid-tier Power 4 programs from coasting on conference prestige over resume-proven Group of 5 or Independent contenders.

Every prior season ($t-1$) is computed with the exact identical game update mechanics, margin limits, and quality multipliers as the active season ($t$), guaranteeing structural parity across time.

Section 1

Single Season & Weekly Game Architecture

Chronological breakdown of each mathematical formula applied during the active season.

1.1 Baseline Elo Initialization

$$\text{Pure Team Elo}_0 = 1500.0$$

Detailed Explanation

Every FBS program starts Week 1 at exactly $1500.0$ on-field Elo. In Version 3.0, Pure Score is defined strictly as Pure Team Elo. No conference foundation points are added. This ensures that early wins against FCS or low-tier teams do not inherit artificial league boosts.

Concrete Example

Before kickoff of Week 1, Georgia (SEC), Indiana (Big Ten), BYU (Big 12), and James Madison (Sun Belt) all start with $\text{Pure Team Elo} = 1500.0$.

1.2 Decoupled Win Expectancy Probability

$$E_H = \frac{1}{1 + 10^{\frac{[\text{Elo}_A + \delta_{\text{Away}}(A)] - [\text{Elo}_H + \delta_{\text{Home}}(H)]}{400.0}}}, \quad E_A = 1.0 - E_H$$

Detailed Explanation

Before game kickoff, each team's on-field rating is modified by venue difficulty. The home team receives their venue boost $\delta_H \ge 0.0$, while the visiting team receives their road penalty $\delta_A \le 0.0$. On neutral fields, both $\delta = 0.0$. Win expectancy ($E$) represents the logistic probability of victory.

Concrete Example

Texas Tech ($1583.3$ Elo, away at Arizona $\delta_A = -19.5$) vs. Arizona ($1545.5$ Elo, home $\delta_H = +15.8$).
Effective Ratings: Texas Tech $= 1563.8$, Arizona $= 1561.3$.
Exponent: $(1563.8 - 1561.3) / 400.0 = 2.5 / 400.0 = 0.00625$. $10^{0.00625} = 1.0145$.
$E_{\text{Arizona}} = 1.0 / (1.0 + 1.0145) = \mathbf{0.4964}$ (49.64% expected win), $E_{\text{TTU}} = \mathbf{0.5036}$ (50.36%).

1.3 Opponent Quality Volatility Multiplier ($K_{\text{adj}}$)

$$K_{\text{adj}} = \text{clamp}\left(1.0 + \frac{\text{Elo}_{\text{Opp}} - 1500.0}{600.0}, \; 0.65, \; 1.35\right)$$

Detailed Explanation

To eliminate circular feedback loops while rewarding schedule strength, opponent quality is captured chronologically at kickoff. Beating an elite program ($>1500$) increases game volatility up to $1.35\times$. Beating an FCS or bottom-tier opponent scales volatility down to $0.65\times$.

Concrete Example

Matchup A: Beating Georgia ($1680.0$ Elo): $K_{\text{adj}} = 1.0 + (1680 - 1500) / 600 = 1.0 + 0.30 = \mathbf{1.300\times}$.
Matchup B: Beating an FCS team ($1200.0$ Elo): $K_{\text{adj}} = 1.0 + (1200 - 1500) / 600 = 1.0 - 0.50 = 0.50 \rightarrow \mathbf{0.650\times}$ (clamped floor).

1.4 1-Point Base Logarithmic Margin Multiplier

$$\text{Mult} = \text{clamp}\left(1.00 + 0.25 \cdot \frac{\ln(|\Delta\text{Score}|)}{\ln(45.0)}, \; 1.00, \; 1.25\right)$$

Detailed Explanation

A 1-point victory yields $\ln(1)/\ln(45) = 0$, guaranteeing an unscaled baseline multiplier of exactly $1.000$. A 21-point victory scales to $1.200$, and blowouts of 45+ points are capped at $1.250$ to discourage unsportsmanlike score running.

Concrete Example

A 14-point win ($\vert{}\Delta\vert{} = 14$): $\ln(14) / \ln(45) = 2.63906 / 3.80666 = 0.69327$.
$\text{Mult} = 1.00 + 0.25(0.69327) = \mathbf{1.1733}$.

1.5 Symmetrical Updates ($K=64.0$, No Loss Floor)

$$\Delta \text{Elo}_{\text{Win}} = \max\left(\mathbf{+6.0}, \; +64.0 \cdot K_{\text{adj}} \cdot (1.0 - E_{\text{Winner}}) \cdot \text{Mult}\right)$$ $$\Delta \text{Elo}_{\text{Loss}} = -64.0 \cdot K_{\text{adj}} \cdot (E_{\text{Loser}}) \cdot \text{Mult}$$

Detailed Explanation

To ensure "a win is always a win," every victory is guaranteed a minimum gain of $+6.0$ points. The penalty for losing is perfectly symmetrical to the base win coefficient ($K=64.0$). Losses are mathematically unbounded with no artificial floor, ensuring expected losses to elite teams naturally approach 0 points.

Concrete Example

Texas Tech defeats Sam Houston as a 92% favorite ($E = 0.92$, $1-E = 0.08$) by 30 points ($\text{Mult} = 1.223$, $K_{\text{adj}} = 0.65$):
Raw win formula $= 64.0 \times 0.65 \times 0.08 \times 1.223 = +4.07$ points.
Clamped win update $= \max(+6.0, +4.07) = \mathbf{+6.0\text{ Elo points}}$ (protected by win floor).

1.6 Two-Stage Prior Sunset Curve ($W_{\text{Prior}}$)

$$\text{Regular Season (Weeks 1–12):} \quad W_{\text{Prior}}(w) = 0.05 + 0.30 \cdot \left(\frac{12 - w}{11}\right)^{2.0}$$ $$\text{Postseason Glide (Weeks 12–16):} \quad W_{\text{Prior}}(w) = 0.05 \cdot \left(\frac{16 - w}{4}\right)^{1.5}, \quad W_{\text{Prior}}(\ge 16) = 0.0\%$$

Detailed Explanation

Stage 1 drops convexly from 35.0% in Week 1 to a 5.0% floor in Week 12. Stage 2 slowly glides that remaining 5.0% down to 0.0% by Week 16 (National Championship Game).

Concrete Example

Week 6 ($w=6$): $W_{\text{Prior}} = 0.05 + 0.30 \cdot (6/11)^2 = 0.05 + 0.30(0.2975) = 0.05 + 0.089 = \mathbf{13.9\%}$ (On-field weight $= \mathbf{86.1\%}$).

1.7 Master Composite Score, SOS Offset & Loss Tax

$$\text{Offset}_{\text{sos}} = 0.10 \times (\text{Avg}_{\text{opponents}} - 1500.0)$$ $$\text{Composite Score} = (1.0 - W_{\text{Prior}}(w)) \cdot (\text{Pure Elo}) + W_{\text{Prior}}(w) \cdot (\text{Regressed Prior}) + \text{Offset}_{\text{sos}}$$ $$\text{Full Power Score} = \text{Composite Score} \times \left(1.0 - 0.0075 \cdot \text{Losses}\right)$$

Detailed Explanation

Combines the active on-field Elo, the decaying Regressed Prior, and an unclamped Strength of Schedule offset ($0.10 \times (\text{SOS} - 1500.0)$), which is also rendered as a dedicated column in the table. The composite score is then deflated by 0.75% for each loss suffered.

Concrete Example

BYU in Week 6 (5–0 record, Pure Elo $= 1589.4$, Regressed Prior $= 1628.5$, SOS $= 1492.6$, $W_{\text{Prior}} = 0.139$):
• On-Field Term: $(1 - 0.139) \times 1589.4 = 0.861 \times 1589.4 = \mathbf{1368.47}$
• Prior Term: $0.139 \times 1628.5 = \mathbf{226.36}$
• SOS Offset: $0.10 \times (1492.6 - 1500.0) = 0.10 \times (-7.4) = \mathbf{-0.74}$
• Composite Score: $1368.47 + 226.36 - 0.74 = \mathbf{1594.09}$
• Loss Tax ($0$ losses): $1594.09 \times 1.0 = \mathbf{1594.1\text{ Full Power Score}}$.

Detailed Explanation

To eliminate browser freezing and allow instantaneous range calculations, the build script compiles every completed historical season into a centralized JSON index containing four audited variables: season-ending power score, wins, losses, and average opponent SOS.

2.2 Dynamic Window Range Formulas

$$\text{Era Average Score} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{y=Y_{\text{start}}}^{Y_{\text{end}}} \text{Score}(y)$$ $$\text{Cumulative Win \%} = \frac{\sum W_y}{\sum W_y + \sum L_y} \times 100\%$$ $$\text{Era Average SOS} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{y=Y_{\text{start}}}^{Y_{\text{end}}} \text{SOS}(y)$$

Detailed Explanation

When a user selects Start Year ($Y_{\text{start}}$) and End Year ($Y_{\text{end}}$), the client calculates arithmetic means across all sampled seasons. The client also identifies the team's Peak Season ($\max \text{Score}_y$) and Lowest Season ($\min \text{Score}_y$).

Section 3

Conference Baselines & Offseason Seeding

Multi-horizon temporal tracking and dual-anchor regression seeding before Week 1.

3.1 Previous Season Pure Finish ($\text{Score}_{t-1}$)

$$\text{Score}_{t-1} = \text{Pure Team Elo}_{t-1}$$

Detailed Explanation

In Version 3.0, the starting seed from the preceding year is strictly the program's final on-field Pure Elo. No conference points are added, ensuring the offseason bridge begins from authentic achievement.

3.2 Dual-Anchor Mean Regression Equations

$$\text{Anchor}_i = 0.60 \cdot \bar{E}_{\text{4yr}}(i) + 0.40 \cdot (\text{Conf Base}_{\text{4yr}})$$ $$R_i = \text{clamp}\left(0.28 - 0.035 \cdot \left(\frac{\bar{E}_{\text{4yr}} - 1500}{100}\right) + 0.050 \cdot \left(\frac{\vert{}\text{Score}_{t-1} - \bar{E}_{\text{4yr}}\vert{}}{100}\right), \; 0.18, \; 0.42\right)$$ $$\text{Regressed Prior}_i = (1.0 - R_i) \cdot \text{Score}_{t-1} + R_i \cdot \text{Anchor}_i$$

Detailed Explanation

Programs are regressed toward a 60/40 blend of their 4-year finish and conference strength. Blue-blood programs with high 4-year floors receive low regression rates ($18\%$), protecting their elite status. Outlier single-season spikes are regressed up to $42\%$ toward reality.

Section 4

Decoupled Venue Splits & Worked Proof

Stadium advantage derivation from 4-year differentials and full upset arithmetic proof.

4.1 Venue Split Equations ($\delta_{\text{Home}}$ & $\delta_{\text{Away}}$)

$$\text{Spread}_i = \text{Avg Margin}_{\text{Home}}(i) - \text{Avg Margin}_{\text{Away}}(i)$$ $$w_{\text{Away}} = \frac{\vert{}\text{Avg Margin}_{\text{Away}}(i)\vert{}}{\vert{}\text{Avg Margin}_{\text{Home}}(i)\vert{} + \vert{}\text{Avg Margin}_{\text{Away}}(i)\vert{}}, \quad w_{\text{Home}} = 1.0 - w_{\text{Away}}$$ $$\delta_{\text{Home}}(i) = \text{clamp}\left(16.0 \cdot \left[1.0 + (\text{Spread}_i - 8.0) \cdot w_{\text{Home}} \cdot 0.25\right], \; 0.0, \; 40.0\right)$$ $$\delta_{\text{Away}}(i) = \text{clamp}\left(-16.0 \cdot \left[1.0 + (\text{Spread}_i - 8.0) \cdot w_{\text{Away}} \cdot 0.25\right], \; -50.0, \; 0.0\right)$$

4.2 Worked Proof: Vanderbilt 40, Alabama 35

Matchup: Alabama (Away) at Vanderbilt (Home) | Final Margin: +5 points
• Effective Home Elo: Vandy $1450.0 + \delta_H(+4.8) = \mathbf{1454.8}$
• Effective Away Elo: Alabama $1674.0 + \delta_A(-18.9) = \mathbf{1655.1}$
• Win Expectancy Exponent: $(1655.1 - 1454.8) / 400 = 0.50075 \rightarrow 10^{0.50075} = 3.1677$
• Win Expectancies: $E_{\text{Vandy}} = 1.0 / (1 + 3.1677) = \mathbf{0.2399}$ (24.0%), $E_{\text{Bama}} = \mathbf{0.7601}$ (76.0%)
• 1-Point Margin Multiplier: $\text{Mult} = 1.00 + 0.25 \cdot \frac{\ln(5)}{\ln(45)} = 1.00 + 0.25(0.4228) = \mathbf{1.1057}$
• Opponent Scalers: $K_{\text{adj}}(\text{Vandy vs Bama}) = 1.0 + (1674 - 1500)/600 = \mathbf{1.290}$
• Opponent Scalers: $K_{\text{adj}}(\text{Bama vs Vandy}) = 1.0 + (1450 - 1500)/600 = \mathbf{0.917}$
Vanderbilt Update: +64.0 * 1.290 * (1.0 - 0.2399) * 1.1057 = +69.34 Elo points
Alabama Update: -64.0 * 0.917 * (0.7601) * 1.1057 = -49.33 Elo points
