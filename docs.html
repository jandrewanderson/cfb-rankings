Part 1: The Three Primary Power Scores

Every program has three distinct ratings evaluated concurrently: Pure Score, Score with Prior, and Full Power Score.

A. Pure Score

Zero Prior Bias

The Pure Score measures solely on-field achievement during the active season. Every program begins Week 1 at exactly $1,500.0$ Elo. It combines $80\%$ of the team's current on-field performance with $20\%$ conference baseline strength.

$$\text{Pure Score} = 0.80 \cdot (\text{Pure Team Elo}) + \text{Conf Blend}$$
Starting Baseline: All 134 programs start Week 1 at $\text{Pure Team Elo} = 1500.0$.
Conf Blend Component: Accounts for league schedule strength without relying on preseason rankings ($20\%$ total conference weight).

B. Score w/ Prior

Historical Floor Grounding

The Score w/ Prior grounds early-season volatility against multi-year program floors. Rather than starting at $1,500.0$, a team begins Week 1 seeded at their mathematically derived Regressed Prior.

$$\text{Score w/ Prior} = 0.80 \cdot (\text{Team Elo w/ Prior}) + \text{Conf Blend}$$
Starting Baseline: Seeded at each program's specific offseason mean-regressed prior (e.g., Georgia starts at $1,727.7$, Ohio State at $1,724.1$).
In-Season Game Updates: Wins and losses adjust this rating game-by-game concurrently with the Pure Elo rating.

C. Full Power Score (National Ranking Metric)

Master Dashboard Rank

The Full Power Score is the final value used to rank the 134 FBS programs. It is built in two distinct stages: (1) Dynamic weighting across the calendar, and (2) applying the direct per-loss tax.

$$\text{Composite Score} = W_{\text{PureScore}}(w) \cdot (\text{Pure Score}) + W_{\text{Prior}}(w) \cdot (\text{Score w/ Prior}) + W_{\text{PureElo}}(w) \cdot (\text{Pure Team Elo})$$ $$\text{Full Power Score} = \text{Composite Score} \times \left(1.0 - 0.0075 \cdot \text{Losses}\right)$$
0.75% Loss Tax: Directly penalizes losses. A 10–0 team retains $100\%$ of its rating; a 9–1 team retains $99.25\%$; an 8–2 team retains $98.5\%$. This breaks rating ties in favor of on-field undefeated records.

Part 2: Dynamic Calendar Prior Decay

Preseason priors carry high predictive utility in September but become irrelevant by November. The engine linearly phases priors out as on-field sample size increases.

Across the standard 12-week regular season, the weight assigned to Score w/ Prior linearly sunsets from 35% in Week 1 down to 10% in Week 12. The vacated weight is transferred dynamically into the Pure Score and Pure Elo:

$$W_{\text{Prior}}(w) = \max\left(0.10, \; 0.35 - (w - 1) \cdot \frac{0.25}{11.0}\right)$$ $$W_{\text{PureScore}}(w) = \frac{0.60 \cdot (1.0 - W_{\text{Prior}}(w))}{1.0 - 0.2364}$$ $$W_{\text{PureElo}}(w) = 1.0 - W_{\text{Prior}}(w) - W_{\text{PureScore}}(w)$$

Week 4 28.2% 56.4% 15.4% Conference play begins; on-field results take majority command

Part 3: Derivation of Precalculated Historical Constants

Before Week 1 kicks off, all 134 programs have three frozen parameters precalculated from historical data: Multi-Horizon Conference Anchors, Decoupled Venue Splits, and Offseason Mean-Regression Priors.

1. Multi-Horizon Conference Strength Formula

Conference strength is not an arbitrary tier list. It is calculated by aggregating all non-conference matchups across three separate temporal horizons to balance multi-decade tradition with immediate year-over-year roster strength:

$$\text{Conf Blend} = 0.05 \cdot (\text{Conf}_{\text{4-Yr Base}}) + 0.05 \cdot (\text{Conf}_{\text{1-Yr w/ Prior}}) + 0.10 \cdot (\text{Conf}_{\text{Cur-Yr}})$$

2. Decoupled Venue Splits ($\delta_{\text{Home}}$ & $\delta_{\text{Away}}$)

Stadia do not create equal advantages. Visiting Baton Rouge at night is vastly different from playing in a neutral dome. Each stadium's home boost and road travel penalty are computed from 4-year regular season home and away point margins:

$$\text{Spread}_i = \text{Avg Margin}_{\text{Home}}(i) - \text{Avg Margin}_{\text{Away}}(i)$$ $$w_{\text{Away}} = \frac{|\text{Avg Margin}_{\text{Away}}(i)|}{|\text{Avg Margin}_{\text{Home}}(i)| + |\text{Avg Margin}_{\text{Away}}(i)|}, \quad w_{\text{Home}} = 1.0 - w_{\text{Away}}$$ $$\delta_{\text{Home}}(i) = \text{clamp}\left(16.0 \cdot \left[1.0 + (\text{Spread}_i - 8.0) \cdot w_{\text{Home}} \cdot 0.25\right], \; 0.0, \; 40.0\right)$$ $$\delta_{\text{Away}}(i) = \text{clamp}\left(-16.0 \cdot \left[1.0 + (\text{Spread}_i - 8.0) \cdot w_{\text{Away}} \cdot 0.25\right], \; -50.0, \; 0.0\right)$$
Worked Example: LSU (Tiger Stadium) Host Edge: +56.0

4-Year Home Margin $= +18.4$, 4-Year Road Margin $= +2.2$

Spread = 18.4 - 2.2 = +16.2
w_away = 2.2 / (18.4 + 2.2) = 0.107; w_home = 0.893
d_h = clamp(16 * [1 + (16.2 - 8) * 0.893 * 0.25], 0, 40) = +40.0 (Max Cap)
d_a = clamp(-16 * [1 + (16.2 - 8) * 0.107 * 0.25], -50, 0) = -19.5
Worked Example: Miami FL (Hard Rock Stadium) Host Edge: +16.0

4-Year Home Margin $= +10.2$, 4-Year Road Margin $= +10.4$

Spread = 10.2 - 10.4 = -0.2 (Road performance matches Home)
Spread is below 8.0 neutral floor
d_h = 0.0 (Zero home crowd boost)
d_a = 0.0 (Zero travel penalty)

3. Dual-Anchor Continuous Offseason Mean-Regression

How do we seed team priors without trusting subjective coaches polls or media hype? The engine runs a dual-anchor regression on every program's final score from the preceding season:

$$\text{Anchor}_i = 0.80 \cdot \bar{E}_{\text{4yr}}(i) + 0.20 \cdot (\text{Conf Base})$$ $$R_i = \text{clamp}\left(0.28 - 0.035 \cdot \left(\frac{\bar{E}_{\text{4yr}} - 1500}{100}\right) + 0.050 \cdot \left(\frac{|\text{Score}_{t-1} - \bar{E}_{\text{4yr}}|}{100}\right), \; 0.18, \; 0.42\right)$$ $$\text{Regressed Prior}_i = (1.0 - R_i) \cdot \text{Score}_{t-1} + R_i \cdot \text{Anchor}_i$$
Blue-Blood Protection: Georgia and Ohio State possess high 4-year floors ($\bar{E} > 1750$). Their continuous regression rate $R_i$ drops near the $18\text{--}21\%$ minimum, protecting their elite prior from artificial shrinkage.
Outlier Spikes Regressed: A team finishing far above its 4-year average (e.g., Indiana jumping to an undefeated season) generates a higher regression rate $R_i \approx 40.1\%$, forcing them toward their multi-year anchor to prevent multi-year distorted bias.

Part 4: Single-Game Update Engine & Mathematical Formulation

Exhaustive breakdown of raw formulas, operational parameters, logarithmic margin scaling, and a worked upset trace.

1. The Raw System Equations

For any contest between Home Team $H$ and Away Team $A$, the engine computes win probabilities and rating adjustments according to the following system of four equations:

Equation 4.1: Decoupled Win Expectancy
$$E_H = \frac{1}{1 + 10^{\frac{[\text{Elo}_A + \delta_{\text{Away}}(A)] - [\text{Elo}_H + \delta_{\text{Home}}(H)]}{400.0}}}, \quad E_A = 1.0 - E_H$$
Equation 4.2: Logarithmic Margin Normalization
$$M_{\text{norm}} = \frac{\ln(|\text{Score}_H - \text{Score}_A| + 1)}{\ln(8.0)}$$
Equation 4.3: 70/30 Hybrid Multiplier
$$\text{Mult} = 0.70 + 0.30 \cdot M_{\text{norm}}$$
Equation 4.4: Asymmetric Rating Adjustments
$$\Delta \text{Elo}_{\text{Win}} = +K_{\text{win}} \cdot (1.0 - E_{\text{Winner}}) \cdot \text{Mult}, \quad K_{\text{win}} = 48.0$$ $$\Delta \text{Elo}_{\text{Loss}} = -K_{\text{loss}} \cdot (E_{\text{Loser}}) \cdot \text{Mult}, \quad K_{\text{loss}} = 96.0$$

2. Parameter Definitions & Theoretical Foundations

Parameter Value / Formula Structural Role & Purpose
K_win 48.0 Determines the maximum possible rating points a winning team can acquire from an improbable victory.
K_loss 96.0 (2x K_win) Doubled loss penalty ensures top contenders suffer severe rating drops when upset, preventing multi-loss teams from lingering near #1.
70/30 Split 0.70 + 0.30 * M_norm 70% of the update is anchored strictly to winning the game. 30% scales with point differential, preventing excessive running up of scores.
ln(8.0) Denominator 2.07944 Normalizes a one-touchdown margin (7 points: $\ln(7+1)/\ln(8) = 1.0$) to equal exactly $1.0$, producing an unscaled baseline multiplier of $1.00$.
δ_Home / δ_Away [0.0, +40.0] / [-50.0, 0.0] Adjusts pre-kickoff win expectations based on stadium difficulty. A road team is evaluated against a tougher standard than on a neutral field.

3. Complete Step-by-Step Worked Trace: Vanderbilt vs. Alabama

The following trace demonstrates the exact arithmetic performed by the engine during Vanderbilt's 40–35 upset over Alabama:

Matchup: Alabama (Away) at Vanderbilt (Home) | Final Score: Vanderbilt 40, Alabama 35 (Margin: +5 points)
Step 1: Compute Venue-Adjusted Effective Elo
Vanderbilt Pure Elo: 1450.0 | Home Boost (δHome): +4.8 → Effective Home Elo = 1454.8
Alabama Pure Elo: 1674.0 | Away Penalty (δAway): -18.9 → Effective Away Elo = 1655.1
Step 2: Calculate Win Expectancy Probabilities ($E$)
Exponent = (1655.1 - 1454.8) / 400.0 = 200.3 / 400.0 = 0.50075
10^0.50075 = 3.16773
E_Vandy (Home) = 1.0 / (1.0 + 3.16773) = 1.0 / 4.16773 = 0.2399 (24.0% expected win probability)
E_Bama (Away) = 1.0 - 0.2399 = 0.7601 (76.0% expected win probability)
Step 3: Calculate Logarithmic Margin Multiplier (70/30 Split)
Absolute Score Margin = |40 - 35| = 5 points
M_norm = ln(5 + 1) / ln(8.0) = ln(6.0) / ln(8.0) = 1.79176 / 2.07944 = 0.86166
Mult = 0.70 + 0.30 * 0.86166 = 0.70 + 0.25850 = 0.9585
Step 4: Execute Asymmetric Updates (K_win=48.0, K_loss=96.0)
Vanderbilt Update: +48.0 * (1.0 - 0.2399) * 0.9585 = +48.0 * 0.7601 * 0.9585 = +34.97 Elo points
Alabama Update: -96.0 * (0.7601) * 0.9585 = -69.94 Elo points

The Asymmetry at Work: Because Vanderbilt pulled off an upset as a $24\%$ underdog, they gain $+35.0$ rating points. But because Alabama lost as a $76\%$ road favorite, the doubled loss penalty ($K=96.0$) deducts $-69.9$ points from their rating, pushing them down the rankings.
